Cut Three Prompts and Prove the Trade

On your own. 45 minutes, then 15 more to take it back to your desk. Every command has a copy button.

Time 45 minutes + 15 optional You produce three rewritten instructions, a before-and-after table, and two sentences of findings Committed locally, never pushed Needs Claude Code 2.1.211+, Python, the lab finished
What you are testing. In the lab you watched someone else's three instructions. Now you cut your own. The claim on trial is the one every team argues about without evidence: an instruction can be cut hard without the answer getting worse. You need a third off the tokens with no answer dropping more than a tenth of its score. A big cut that halves the quality is not a win with a nice number on it - it is a regression.
Your three. There are eight instructions in the pack; you work on three. Take set A unless you were told otherwise. Every command below is written for set A - for B or C, swap the three names in.
SetFirstSecondThird
Acode-reviewtest-casesdefect-triage
Bimpact-noterelease-notesexplain-code
Cstage-summaryonboarding-planimpact-note

Each one has a scoring guide of its own, and each writes its answer to a file so it can be marked. You never edit the originals - your versions live in your own folder.

3Cut them 13-27 min

Why. This is the skill. Everything else today is measurement; this is the judgement the measurement exists to settle.

Six ways to cut. Use at least three on each one. Reach for the top of the list first - that is where the tokens usually are.

CutBecause
1 Point at a file instead of pasting itClaude can open it itself, and pasted text is paid for again on every retry
2 Name the part, not the wholeA section, or a heading to search for, instead of a document or a folder
3 Give a shape, not a paragraphA column list beats three sentences of "first I would like you to"
4 Cap the answerOutput is the expensive half. A summary, an explanation, and a chat recap of the file it just wrote is three payments for one answer
5 Drop examples the shape already coversAn example earns its cost only when the format cannot be described more cheaply
6 Cut the flattery and the repeatsThe role paragraph, the hedging, and every "again, please make sure"

b. The ten-minute loop, for your own work. Take an instruction you actually use - a review, a ticket write-up, a test plan. It does not need to be about any of today's material.

MinutesDo this
0-2Write down the five things a good answer must contain, one line each. That is your scoring guide - the hard part, and the part nobody does
2-5Run it as it stands and record what it cost
5-8Cut it with three of the six techniques; run it again
8-10Have a stronger model mark both answers against your five things, and read the evidence lines rather than the totals

The three scripts you used today - the runner, the ledger and the scorer - need only Python and Claude Code, so they copy into any repository. The scorer finds a guide by file name; point it at your own naming and it works unchanged.

If you finish early. Run one rewrite three times and score each. The spread between identical runs is your noise floor - and any improvement smaller than it is not a finding. Next week uses that same idea to decide when a cheaper model is good enough.

Passing bar

A third off the tokens across your three, and no answer below nine tenths of its starting score. Every score given by a stronger model than wrote the answer, with one row checked by you. Every rewrite carries a note of what you cut and why. The before-and-after page built once and extended twice, never regenerated. Two sentences of findings, the first with a number in it. All committed locally.
